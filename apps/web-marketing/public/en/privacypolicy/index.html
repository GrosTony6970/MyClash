1.1 Two different roles, depending on the data

This distinction matters, because it determines who you should talk to:

SituationWho is the data controller
You created a MyClash account (email address, password, profile, follows, notification preferences) LYON AMHE — contact us directly
An event organiser entered you on their roster (competitor, referee, instructor, volunteer), whether or not you have an account The organiser of that event. MyClash acts as their processor: we hold and process that data on their instructions

If your data reached MyClash through an event you took part in, the organiser of that event is your first point of contact. We will always help you reach them, and we act ourselves whenever the request concerns the platform.

2.6 Assistance features (AI)

If an organiser has enabled the AI features (see §7), the questions asked and the content generated are recorded, together with the volume consumed. See §8 for what leaves our infrastructure.

2.7 Technical data

The IP address and browser of a guest session and of an acceptance record, server logs, and error reports. We do not perform audience measurement: MyClash contains no analytics tool, no advertising tag, and no third-party tracker. We do not record which pages you visit or how long you spend on them.

2.8 What we never collect

No special-category data within the meaning of Article 9 GDPR (health, ethnic origin, political or religious opinions, biometric data), no bank or payment details, and no precise geolocation.

3. Being on MyClash without ever having signed up

This is the most distinctive part of the platform, and it deserves a section of its own.

To run a competition, an organiser enters their roster: everyone taking part, with the details listed in §2.1. You can therefore exist on MyClash without ever having created an account, because the organiser needed to register you in order to run their event.

In that situation:

  • You may claim your profile. A link sent to the email address the organiser registered proves it is you, and the profile becomes yours.
  • You may use a guest session. Finding your own name on an event's roster opens a limited session — your schedule, your bouts, your workshops — with no account and no password. It is a cookie tied to that event which expires seven days after the event ends.
  • You may object. Write to the organiser, or to us; see §11.

5. What is public, and what never is

Our rule is simple, and it is written down as a project decision: what is public online is what would be visible at the venue. A HEMA competition is public — names are announced, brackets are on the wall, results are read out.

DataVisibility
Name, club, countryPublic on results and brackets
Scores, rankings, bracket position, penalties receivedPublic
Exchange-by-exchange detailPublic at event level; aggregated in statistics
Profile photoVisible to signed-in users; an organiser may make an event fully public
Your personal schedule, your workshop attendanceVisible only to you and to the organiser
Email addressNever public. No public interface returns it; the database itself enforces this
Date of birth, notes, technical dataNever public

You can restrict some of this yourself in your settings: hide your workshops, refuse to be followed, keep your email hidden from the people who follow you. Publication of your results is not currently a self-service setting — if you want your name withdrawn from public results, ask us and we will anonymise it (see §11).

6.1 What happens when you delete your account

Deletion is immediate. There is no waiting period and no way to reverse it. The moment you confirm:

  • your login credentials are destroyed and you can no longer sign in;
  • your profile details — email, date of birth, photo, links, notes — are erased;
  • your guest sessions, push subscriptions, follows and preferences are deleted;
  • personal data inside the audit trail is redacted in place.

Your competition results keep your name. A result is the record of a public competition in which you took part, alongside the opponents you faced; erasing it would rewrite other people's history too. If you want your name removed from published results as well, ask us: we anonymise it, and the result survives under an anonymous entry.

We retain a record that an erasure took place and when — never who it concerned. That record is a one-way cryptographic fingerprint, which cannot be traced back to you.

7.1 What Sentry does and does not receive

Sentry receives a technical error log: the fault, the line of code that produced it, the version of the application, and the sequence of pages that led there. It is the tool that tells us the application is broken before you have to.

We have explicitly disabled the collection of personal data by this tool. Concretely, the following are never transmitted:

  • your IP address;
  • your cookies and your session;
  • the content of what you submit (forms, scores, messages);
  • your identity — we never attach an account to an error report.

On top of that, the diagnostic details our servers add to a report pass through a filter that removes email addresses, authentication tokens and any value bearing the name of a secret.

One detail, for completeness: an error log contains the address of the page where it occurred, and some MyClash addresses include the name of a public profile — for instance /fighters/jean-dupont. That information is the same as what the page itself displays publicly. Nothing private travels with it.

7.2 HEMA Ratings

MyClash reads public ratings from hemaratings.com to enrich fighter profiles. We send nothing automatically. An organiser may export their results in the HEMA Ratings format, but it is the organiser who chooses to submit that file, under their own responsibility.

9. Cookies

MyClash sets four cookies, all our own. None is used for advertising, audience measurement or tracking.

CookiePurposeLifetime
sb-access-tokenKeeps you signed inSession, renewed while you stay active
sb-refresh-tokenRenews your session without asking you to sign in again30 days
mc_guestYour guest session at an event, when you have no accountUntil 7 days after the event
mc_localeRemembers whether you chose French or English1 year

This is why you will not see a cookie banner. A consent banner is required for cookies that are not necessary — advertising, analytics, third-party trackers. MyClash sets none. The first three cookies are strictly necessary for a service you asked for by signing in; the fourth records a choice you made yourself. Displaying a consent dialog that decides nothing would only teach you to dismiss the ones that matter.

If MyClash ever adds a measurement tool, this section changes and a real consent mechanism will come with it — one that blocks the tool until you agree.

10. Security

  • All traffic is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS).
  • Passwords are never stored in clear text.
  • Access to your data is enforced by the database itself, not merely by the application.
  • Session cookies are inaccessible to JavaScript, which neutralises an entire class of attack.
  • Backups are encrypted and stored off-site.
  • Sign-in attempts are rate-limited, and administrative actions are recorded in an audit trail.
  • MyClash is open-source software: its security can be inspected by anyone rather than taken on trust.

12. Changes to this policy

Each version of this policy carries a date, and MyClash records which version you accepted.
